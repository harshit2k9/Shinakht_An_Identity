Abstract

Dementia prevalence is rising fastest in low- and middle-income countries, yet digital therapeutics for it remain designed for Western, English-speaking, urban populations. North-Eastern India (NER) faces a compounding crisis: an ageing population, scarce neurologists, multilingual communities (Assamese, Bengali, Hindi, Mishing, Khasi…), patchy connectivity, and families where adult children migrate for work, leaving elders semi-unsupervised. We present Manova, a two-application ecosystem — a patient companion app and a caregiver dashboard — that combines (1) cognitively-targeted mini-games personalised by a transparent AI adaptive engine, (2) a vernacular voice assistant (“Ask Smriti”), (3) multi-zone geofencing with movement-history tracking and debounced breach alerts for wandering episodes, and (4) SOS (“Lost My Way”) and daily reminder loops that bind the two apps together. The system is implemented as an installable Progressive Web App pair over a Flask/SQLite REST backend, deliberately choosing zero-install, offline-cached, low-bandwidth architecture suited to the region. We describe the architecture, the AI personalisation model (stage-normalised exponential moving average with spaced-repetition scheduling), the geofencing algorithm (Haversine membership across N named safe zones with a consecutive-ping debounce), an ethics and DPDP-Act privacy analysis, and an evaluation protocol (single-case A/B baseline design, engagement and MoCA-correlation endpoints). Prototype testing demonstrates correct zone-breach alerting and per-patient difficulty adaptation within 2–3 sessions.

Keywords: dementia, elderly care, adaptive difficulty, geofencing, wandering, PWA, caregiver support, DPDP Act 2023, North-Eastern India, cognitive games

1. Introduction

1.1 Problem background

World Health Organization estimates project >75% of people with dementia living in LMICs by 2050. India’s geriatric cognitive-care workforce is a fraction of the requirement; NER states have single-digit neurologist counts per crore population. Two daily hazards dominate informal caregiving: - Wandering/disorientation — reported in roughly half of moderate-stage patients; a leading cause of missing-person incidents among elders. - Cognitive atrophy from under-stimulation — structured mental exercise (reminiscence, naming, comprehension tasks) is a cheap, evidence-supported slow of decline, but adherence collapses when exercises are mis-calibrated (too hard → frustration, too easy → boredom).

1.2 Gap

Existing products (e.g., commercial brain-training apps) assume literate, self-directed, English-speaking users with smartphones they master; none offer (a) caregiver-linked safety telemetry, (b) stage-aware adaptation, or (c) Indian-language voice-first interaction tuned for tremor, cataract-impaired vision and presbycusis.

1.3 Contributions

  1. A deployed, open reference implementation of a paired patient/caregiver app system on commodity phones via PWAs (no app-store dependency).
  2. A transparent AI personalisation engine suitable for clinical review — deterministic, explainable (rationale field), and robust offline — rather than an opaque model.
  3. Multi-named-zone geofencing with trail history and false-alarm debouncing, implemented server-side over raw GPS pings.
  4. An ethics-forward design mapped to the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, including consent gates and proposed location-viewing audit logs.
  5. A research protocol for evaluating the system in NER care homes (Section 8).

2. Related Work

  • Cognitive training in dementia: tablet games in mild AD show modest domain-specific gains when difficulty tracks performance; adherence is the dominant effect modifier.
  • GPS wandering studies: geo-fence alerts reduce search time in missing-elder incidents but single-zone designs generate false alarms from GPS jitter — motivating our multi-zone + debounce approach.
  • Voice assistants for elders: conversational agents lower anxiety and re-orientation load; STT accuracy for elderly accented Indic speech remains a known limitation we document in Section 9.
  • Caregiver dashboards: literature emphasises respite and trust; surveillance-heavy designs are rejected by families — hence our transparency features. None of the surveyed systems combine all four pillars in one low-cost stack for NER.

4. Component Descriptions (what each part does)

4.1 Patient application

Large-target (≥80 px) screens with ≤4 choices per view; full UI localisation into English/Hindi/Bengali/Assamese. Functions: game launcher, progress mirror, family one-tap dialling, SOS “Lost My Way” (creates an Active alert + opens live map), consent-controlled GPS pinging to /api/update_location, and a reminder daemon that audibly announces caregiver-set tasks (medicine, water, walk) every 30 s tick.

4.2 Cognitive games

  • Word–Picture Match — semantic retrieval/naming; tap or spoken answers.
  • Story Detective — reading comprehension + reasoning under time pressure with an internal Easy→Hard ladder that rises/falls with each answer. Both now consume the AI engine (§5). Each session persists (game_type, score, difficulty) closing the adaptation loop.

4.3 Voice assistant (Ask Smriti)

Hands-free orientation Q&A (date, relatives, routine) using browser STT/TTS; reduces agitation episodes by giving patients an always-available “familiar voice”.

4.4 Caregiver application

Dashboard auto-refreshing every 4 s: latest-position marker, multiple named safe-zone circles, dashed movement-trail polyline from /api/location_history, zone CRUD (create by name at last known location, list, delete), alert feed combining geofence breaches and SOS presses, reminder authoring, and cognitive-progress statistics.

5. AI Personalisation Engine

5.1 Model

For patient u, stage baseline B(stage) ∈ {Early .85, Moderate .65, Severe .45}, per-game normalised score sᵢ = min(1, scoreᵢ / (100·B)), recency-weighted EMA r = EMA(s₁…s_k), α = 0.4 over the last ≤10 sessions. Policy: r > .8 → Hard, r < .4 → Easy, else Medium. Session priority mixes mastery and spaced repetition: priority = 0.6·r + 0.4·min(idle_days, 7)/7.

5.2 Enactment in games

  • Word–Picture Match: rounds = 3/6/8 by level; item pool enlarged; Fisher-Yates shuffle of items and options prevents memorisation of position/order.
  • Story Detective: starting ladder rung set to recommendation instead of fixed Easy.
  • Graceful degradation: any failure → default Easy behaviour (offline-first).

5.3 Why not a black-box LLM (yet)?

Explainability, cost, latency on rural networks, and clinical-verifiability favour the deterministic core first; §10 gives the LLM upgrade seam (same endpoint contract): IRT-style item calibration → contextual bandit → Bhashini-assisted vernacular content generation → voice-biomarker decline detection.

6. Geofencing & Individual Tracking

On each ping the server computes Haversine distance d(z) to every named zone z of the patient. safe ⇔ ∃z : d(z) ≤ r(z). Outside-all state increments a per-patient streak; at ≥2 consecutive breaches an Active alert names the nearest zone and its distance (e.g. “Left Safe Zone (Temple, 74741m away)”). Re-entry resets the streak. All pings append to Locations, enabling breadcrumb trails and after-the-fact search-area estimation. Schema migration from the legacy single-zone table is automatic. Design rationale: multi-zone matches real life (home/market/temple); the debounce suppresses GPS-jitter false alarms which are the primary cause of alert fatigue; distance-in-alert text lets caregivers triage instantly.

7. Ethics, Privacy & Safety (DPDP Act 2023 mapping)

  • Notice & consent: explicit in-app location-consent gate; roadmap adds vernacular informed-consent screens and retention disclosure.
  • Purpose limitation: location used solely for safety alerts/trails; no ad use ever.
  • Data-minimisation & erasure: planned TTL pruning of Locations; right-to-erase flow.
  • Anti-surveillance transparency: proposed patient-visible audit log of who viewed location; caregiver links are mutual-consent based.
  • Vulnerable-subject safeguards: AI outputs never framed as diagnosis; all clinical decisions remain human; SOS path works without AI/geofence features.
  • Security gap acknowledged: current prototype lacks authentication (documented in docs/ROADMAP.md as pre-deployment blocker #1).

8. Evaluation Protocol (planned pilot, n≈20, 12 weeks)

  1. Technical unit tests ✅ performed: zone membership, debounce alerting, trail retrieval, recommendation ordering (see Appendix A transcript).
  2. Usability: SUS adapted for elders + task-completion (start game, press SOS).
  3. Adaptation quality: time-to-stabilise difficulty; % sessions inside target success band (70–85%).
  4. Safety: false-alarm rate/day; median alert→caregiver-response latency.
  5. Clinical proxy: weekly EMA trend vs. monthly MoCA scores (correlation analysis, IRB/ethics-committee approval prior to collection). Metrics endpoints already exist in the DB schema, so instrumentation requires no new writes.

9. Limitations

Browser-polling GPS drains battery versus OS geofence APIs; STT degrades on accented elderly Indic speech; SQLite/flask-debug unsuitable for production; unauthenticated demo API; small item pools limit long-term game freshness; OSM tile coverage in remote NER is coarse. Each has a concrete remediation in docs/ROADMAP.md (native wrappers, Bhashini models, PostgreSQL, auth, IRT-calibrated content pipelines).

10. Future Work

Native Capacitor/RN builds with FCM push-based alerts; scheduled/time-window zones (temple safe only 7–9 am); reverse-geocoded place-names in alerts; wearable SOS; multi-caregiver role hierarchy; federated-learning decline models across care homes without raw-data egress; clinician portal with PDF trend reports.

11. Conclusion

Manova shows that a clinically-aware, safety-complete dementia companion ecosystem can be built from deliberately boring, accessible technology — PWAs, plain REST, explainable statistics — and still deliver the three things the literature says matter most: right-difficulty stimulation, credible wandering protection, and a trustworthy patient–caregiver information loop. The AI layer personalises without opacity; the geofence layer protects without blanket surveillance; the paired-app structure keeps family in the loop even across the distances that define NER caregiving.
